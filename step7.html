01 · HIGH
Diagnose the trailer unloading bottleneck
Inbound
Trailer Unloading averages 120 min against a 30 min benchmark — 4× expected. Spikes to 200 min observed. This is the root cause of cascading delays into QC inspection, putaway, and downstream receipt closure.
Recommended action
Analyse unloading times by dock door, shift, and carrier using the WMS event log. Cross-reference with carrier arrival schedules to identify whether delays are pre-dock (carrier) or on-dock (crew, equipment). Review staffing levels and dock equipment availability during peak arrival windows. Define a target lead time and set up WMS alerting for breaches.

3–5 days investigation

02 · HIGH
Reduce outbound SLA failures — focus on picking and packing
Outbound
9.5% of outbound events breach SLA — 1,073 of 11,255 events over 14 days. Picking Completed accounts for 76 rework events and Goods Packed for 52. These two steps alone are responsible for the majority of SLA misses with direct client delivery impact.
Recommended action
Map rework triggers per operator, SKU, and shift using the WMS event log. Review pick instruction quality — check for location accuracy issues, label errors, and scan confirmation gaps. Introduce a packing verification step before consolidation. Target a 50% reduction in rework events within 30 days as a measurable first milestone.
Effort
1–2 weeks
Owner
Outbound team lead
Type
Process + WMS config
03 · HIGH
Investigate the Goods Packed bottleneck — 16.2 min average
Outbound
Goods Packed is the highest lead time step in the outbound flow at 16.2 min average. 52 rework events are concentrated here. This step blocks consolidation and dispatch and is a likely contributor to the overall SLA failure rate.
Recommended action
Conduct a time study of packing stations across shifts. Identify whether the bottleneck is physical (space, materials, equipment) or instruction-based (WMS pack config, carton assignment logic). Compare packing performance across operators to isolate whether this is a training, process, or system issue. Review cartonisation rules in WMS to check if pack sequences are optimised.
Effort
1 week
Owner
Outbound ops manager
Type
Process investigation
04 · HIGH
Diagnose inbound SLA failures — priority receipt routing
Inbound
13.3% of inbound events are late — a higher rate than outbound despite significantly lower volume. Priority inbound receipts fail at 20.5%, suggesting WMS priority routing for inbound is not configured or not functioning correctly. 254 events affected over 14 days.
Recommended action
Audit WMS inbound priority routing configuration. Test whether priority flags are applied correctly at ASN and receipt creation. Review dock door assignment logic to confirm priority arrivals are being directed to the fastest available door. Check whether priority status is being inherited correctly through the inbound flow from ASN through to receipt close.
Effort
3–5 days
Owner
Inbound supervisor + WMS team
Type
WMS configuration
05 · MED
Standardise the QC inspection process — reduce variability
Inbound
QC Inspection averages 63 min with spikes to 122 min. High variability — no consistent inspection pattern visible in the data — suggests inconsistent checklist adherence, staffing gaps, or product-category-specific inspection differences that are not being tracked.
Recommended action
Define a standardised inspection checklist per product category. Set time targets per inspection type and begin tracking actuals in WMS. Analyse variability by shift, inspector, and product type to identify whether the issue is training, process, or category-driven. Review whether the current 150 QC events per 14 days is proportionate to inbound volume and adjust inspection sampling rate if needed.

2–3 weeks
